Canadian investors are ex bankers and often much more conservative compared to US investors who are usually successful founders.
### Think like an investor
Typical private company valuations: 1 - 3x (gross) revenue of previous year (”trailing”); Or 10 –20x trailing EBITDA (“trailing earnings”)
But public valuations are even higher: the average price to earnings of the S&P 500 is 38:1  

The “Founder’s Dilemma” – accepting venture money implies: • Specific path and timeline to “exit” (sale of the business). 
- Founding CEO -> at risk
- The CEO does not necessarily run the show anymore. The venture investors join the board 

Venture Investors seek to: 
- Identify investment opportunities that have the potential to generate superior (target) returns: 10x (40% IRR) for an early-stage deal. 
- Use portfolio theory: can’t predict the outcome of one specific deal or company – multiple risks can come into play. 
- Make decisions with limited information and limited outcome influence. 
- Value add: performance above some “hurdle rate” of alternative investment (public equity performance, commercial paper).

Why are per-company investment targets so high (> 10x / deal)?
Answer: They Need to Be… Fund return is a function of outcomes of “pooled” investments. Consider fund with 10 investments. 
After 5 years: 
- 7 investments (companies) are dead (100% loss). 
- 2 investments are “walking wounded” (-50% to +100% potential return). 
- 1 investment needs to be a “bonanza” – 1,000 to 10,000% return. 
Winning investment must provide a return that pays for the dead and walking wounded investments as well as itself, amortized over the 5-year period.
