Peter Drucker identified seven sources of innovation as the unexpected, incongruities, process needs, industry and market changes, demographic changes, changes in perception, and new knowledge. These sources are opportunities for creating purposeful, focused change within an enterprise to improve its economic or social potential

### The seven sources of innovation
- **The unexpected:** 
    Innovations can arise from unexpected successes, failures, or external events. 
- **Incongruities:** 
    This refers to the gaps between what is assumed to be true and what is actually the case, or between what is and what "ought to be". 
- **Process needs:** 
    Innovations can be developed to address shortcomings or needs in a particular process. 
- **Industry and market changes:** 
    Shifts in industry structures or market dynamics present opportunities for innovation. 
- **Demographic changes:** 
    Changes in population characteristics, such as age, income, or education, can create new needs and opportunities. 
- **Changes in perception:** 
    Innovations can be driven by shifts in how people perceive things, even if the underlying reality hasn't changed.
- **New knowledge:** 
    Innovations often emerge from advancements in scientific, technical, or social knowledge.
