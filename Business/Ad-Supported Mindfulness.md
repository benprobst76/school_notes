CalmQuest, a mindfulness app, has grown steadily among students and young professionals. The founder wants to accelerate growth and is considering a new engagement model: users earn rewards for maintaining meditation streaks. Rewards include “loot boxes” with cosmetic themes for the app and occasional entries into small cash-prize draws. Ads and optional microtransactions would fund the model. The idea is to make mindfulness “fun” and encourage consistency. Early tests show that streak-based features increase session frequency, but some users spend hours meditating in one day to keep streaks alive. Others skip breaks or stack sessions to unlock rewards faster. The founder sees this as strong engagement, but mental health advisors caution that gamified incentives might change why people meditate, and regulators have flagged concerns about apps using randomized rewards similar to gaming mechanics. CalmQuest collects detailed engagement data (e.g. session length, streak patterns, and reward redemption) and plans to use algorithms to personalize offers. The founder wants to ensure the model drives healthy habits, not harmful ones, and is exploring ways to monitor early warning signs before problems scale.
### Question
What two potential risks could emerge from this engagement mode and why are they potential risks? Propose one metric or trigger the app could monitor to detect harm early and why this metric or trigger would be effective at detecting harm.
### Answer
- One potential risk is that users will end up spending too much time on CalmQuest for the sake of earning points and rewards. This would be a risk because this goes against the frame of mindfulness and instead results in more use of a mobile app. 
- Another risk is that the gamification of the app will cause users to meditate for the points rather then supporitng mindfullness
