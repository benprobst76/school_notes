FreshFork is a Kingston-based meal kit start-up that sources meat and produce from regional farms and specialty items from Toronto wholesalers. Meals kits are ordered through an online website, assembled by food safety certified staff in a small industrial kitchen that is rented by the founder and then delivered to customers home by in-house staff and gig drivers to manage fluctuating demand. FreshFork thrived during the pandemic when remote work drove demand for home cooking, and their local and sustainable menus were well received by customers who chose their company over the larger national brands that offered more consistent offerings and pricing but lacked the local and/or sustainable focus. By mid-2025, however, orders have dropped sharply as many customers return to offices and prefer dining out. Therefore, the founder is considering a pivot to office-catering subscriptions: small businesses would sign up for recurring lunch deliveries (e.g., two or three times per week or month) with set menus and predictable pricing, rather than placing one-off catering orders. In Kingston, hundreds of small firms order catering from local restaurants or national chains like Subway (which offer loyalty discounts and easy app-based ordering) for team lunches, client meetings, and staff appreciation days. Usage varies widely: some offices book catering only for special occasions, while others schedule weekly or biweekly lunches to encourage in-office attendance. On average, businesses that use catering purchase food for 8 to 10 people at a time and spend about $150–$250 per order, with frequency ranging widely from once a quarter to several times per week. On days that their office does not supply lunch, employees often bring lunch from home or grab food from nearby cafés. The catering market in Kingston is crowded with at least a dozen local providers competing aggressively on price. Office managers say budgets are tight and they value predictable pricing and reliable delivery windows.
### Question
Using [[Porter’s Five Forces]], identify the two most important forces shaping the attractiveness of the office-catering pivot for FreshFork and explain why these forces matter most. Then recommend one defensible entry choice based on your analysis.
### Answer
The most important forces shaping the attractiveness of the pivot are; Threat of Substitutes and Buyer Power. 
There is a threat of substitution because many of their customers have returned to office and are now choosing to dine out instead of ordering meal kits. This matters most because customers are choosing one or the other and the more that choose to dine out the less business FreshFork makes.
The buyer has bargaining power because there is little differentiation between competitors in terms of meal kits. FreshFork was using there local and sustainable practices to differentiate, but that means they can't compete on price. Customers in this market are more likely to be sensitive to price because there is little differences between meal kits and low or no cost to changing so they can easily substitute to a cheaper competitor. 

As a defensible barrier I would recommend to FreshFork is to focus on a niche that would differentiate themselves from the competition. For example FreshFork could only focus on catering to startups and offer caffeine or late night options for startups where employees are working long hours.
