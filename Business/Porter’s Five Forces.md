Porter's five forces is a model used to analyze the competitive intensity and therefore the profitability of an industry. The five forces are ==threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of buyers, bargaining power of suppliers, and competitive rivalry among existing firms==. A business can use this framework to understand its competitive environment and develop a strategy

### Threat of new entrants
The likelihood that new competitors will enter the market, which can decrease a company's market share. Factors like high barriers to entry (e.g., high startup costs or regulations) can reduce this threat.
### Threat of substitutes
The possibility that customers will switch to a different type of product or service that fulfills the same need.
### Bargaining power of buyers
The ability of customers to put pressure on companies to lower prices or improve quality.
### Bargaining power of suppliers
The ability of suppliers to increase prices or reduce the quality of the goods and services they provide.
### Competitive rivalry
The intensity of competition among existing companies in the same industry. This is influenced by factors like the number of competitors, industry growth rates, and product differentiation.
